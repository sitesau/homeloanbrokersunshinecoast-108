A strong broker conversation separates figures that are easy to blur. The first is calculator output, which is only an indicative starting point. The second is lender assessment after serviceability checks, where income, debts, dependants and existing commitments change the result. The third is household comfort: the repayment level that still leaves room for rates, insurance, maintenance and life events.

  • Calculator figure: useful for scope, not commitment.
  • Lender figure: depends on verified income, liabilities and policy.
  • Liveable figure: the repayment the household can sustain after settlement.

Who this applies to

The published borrower scenarios are first home buyers, refinancers, investors and construction borrowers. The common need is structure before a lender is chosen, a contract is signed or discharge paperwork begins.

  • First home buyers: test treatment of the Queensland First Home Owner Grant, the Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme, genuine savings and any deposit gap.
  • Refinancers: compare the new rate with fees, loan split, loan purpose and whether the switch clears break or discharge costs.
  • Investors: ask how interest only lending, offset use, multiple property lending and SMSF structures affect lender appetite.
  • Construction borrowers: confirm staged funding evidence, valuation steps and settlement timing.

These are file questions, not suburb claims. Examples tied to Maroochydore, Caloundra and Buderim should be read as prompts for different purchase pressures rather than proof of special local rules.

Local questions that change the file

Local detail matters only when it changes assessment or timing. Before settlement pressure builds, ask about property type, contract dates, conveyancer coordination, lender valuation and reliance on a grant or scheme. If the home is not chosen yet, request scenarios across more than one price point. If refinancing, ask for current loan fees beside the proposed structure, not only the headline rate.

Investment files need an extra test: whether the setup still works if rent or existing debt is treated conservatively. Construction files need document timing for staged releases. First buyer files need the deposit and grant position tested early enough to avoid a weak pre approval.

  • Which income types were counted, and which were discounted?
  • Are dependants, commitments and existing limits fully loaded?
  • What changes if rates rise, rent is shaded or the build runs long?
  • Which tasks sit with lender, conveyancer and broker before settlement?

Fees, commission and written disclosure

Published guidance says a standard home loan review has no direct borrower fee, while mortgage brokers are generally paid commission by the lender when a loan settles. It also says commission, any fees and clawback arrangements must be disclosed in writing before the borrower commits.

That disclosure should answer who pays whom, whether a fee could arise in unusual circumstances, and what may happen if the loan is repaid or refinanced quickly. ASIC Moneysmart also tells home loan borrowers to compare features, fees and repayments rather than the interest rate alone.

  • Which lenders were considered and why were some excluded?
  • Why did the recommended lender make the shortlist?
  • What upfront and ongoing fees apply?
  • Is offset or redraw included, and what flexibility is lost?
  • What trade off exists between rate, features and approval fit?

Documents and the decision point

Common file basics are two recent payslips, three months of personal account statements, identification, current loan or rent details and an idea of the property type. Having these ready moves the conversation from general chat to a file that can be tested against lender policy.

Each borrower type has a different decision point. First home buyers often need to know whether deposit, grant and scheme eligibility support the target price. Refinancers need evidence that savings or structure justify moving lenders. Investors need the loan setup to match the intended hold, cash flow and future borrowing room. Construction borrowers need staged funding evidence aligned with valuation and settlement timing.

A useful review ends with the next document request, the lender shortlist, the reason each option was included and the settlement tasks assigned across lender, conveyancer and broker.

  1. Prepare the file basics. Collect income evidence, bank statements, identification, current rent or loan details and the intended property type before the review.
  2. Test borrowing power properly. Use the review to compare calculator output with lender specific serviceability rather than relying on a generic estimate.
  3. Compare the shortlist. Ask why each lender is included, what fees apply, which features are present and where rate, flexibility and approval fit trade off.
  4. Confirm disclosure and settlement tasks. Get commission, fees and clawback in writing, then list the remaining steps across lender, conveyancer and broker.
